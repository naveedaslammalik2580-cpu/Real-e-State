How to Calculate Real Estate Commission


Start with the gross commission on the transaction. Multiply the property sale price by the negotiated percentage and divide by 100.


Total Commission = Sale Price × Commission Rate ÷ 100


A real estate agent commission calculator goes further: agent take-home commission can differ from gross commission because of the agent’s role, brokerage split, referral fees and other deductions. Quick Commission shows the gross amount; Agent Earnings shows those additional steps.

How Real Estate Commission Splits Work


The listing-side allocation determines how much of the commission remainder goes to the listing side; the buyer side receives the rest. Your role selects one side or both. The agent-brokerage split then applies to your applicable side, rather than automatically to the whole transaction commission.


70% agent30% brokerage


A 70/30 split is an illustration, not a universal arrangement. Use this commission split calculator with your own agreement. The calculator deducts referral and franchise fees before these allocations, and the transaction fee afterward.


Understanding Real Estate Agent Fees


Agents, team members and brokers can use Agent Earnings to estimate income under this specific model. Real estate agent fees and the order in which they apply vary by agreement.

How Much Does a Seller Receive After Commission?


The Seller Proceeds calculator estimates what remains from the sale price after the entered commission and optional costs. It assumes the full commission you enter is deducted from the seller’s sale proceeds.


Estimated seller proceeds = Sale price − Entered commission − Other closing costs − Mortgage payoff


For a $450,000 sale at 3%, 1.5% other closing costs and a $200,000 mortgage payoff: $450,000 − $13,500 − $6,750 − $200,000 = $229,750. With no other costs or payoff, the same sale gives $436,500. These seller cost inputs differ from the zero-cost defaults.


Real estate selling costs can include expenses or taxes beyond this model. Proceeds are not taxable profit, and this estimate is not a complete settlement statement. Avoid including commission twice in other closing costs.


Compensation is negotiable and not set by law. Sellers do not necessarily pay every agent’s commission. Buyers can negotiate a seller contribution toward their agent’s compensation, subject to the agreements. See NAR’s guide to written buyer agreements and guide to offers of compensation for the payment and agreement context.


Common Commission Calculation Mistakes


  • Confusing gross commission with earnings. Your role, split and fees determine what you keep.
  • Applying a split to the wrong amount. Use your applicable side after the modeled percentage deductions.
  • Forgetting fees. Check referral, franchise and transaction fees against your agreement.
  • Assuming rates are fixed. Use negotiated compensation; example rates are not standards.
  • Confusing proceeds with taxable profit. A tax calculation needs additional information.
  • Omitting selling expenses. Include the mortgage payoff and applicable closing costs when estimating seller proceeds.


Frequently Asked Questions
How is real estate commission calculated?
Multiply the property sale price by the negotiated commission rate and divide by 100. Agent earnings then depend on the applicable side, fees and brokerage split.


How much is a 5% commission on a $400,000 home?
$400,000 × 5 ÷ 100 = $20,000 in total commission. This is an illustrative rate, not a recommended or mandatory rate.


Are real estate commissions negotiable?
Yes. Real estate commissions are negotiable and are not set by law. The 3% default and other example rates on this page are illustrative inputs; use the compensation in your agreement.


What is a 70/30 real estate commission split?
It allocates 70% of the relevant commission amount to the agent and 30% to the brokerage. In this calculator, that amount is your selected side after referral and franchise deductions. The transaction fee is subtracted afterward. This is one example arrangement, not a universal split.


Who pays real estate agent commission?
Payment depends on the compensation agreements. Buyers may be responsible under their buyer agreement and may negotiate a contribution from the seller or another agent. Sellers do not necessarily pay all commissions. Seller Proceeds here assumes the entire commission entered is deducted from the seller’s sale price. See the NAR references below.


How much commission does a real estate agent actually keep?
It depends on the agreement, role and fees. With this page’s defaults, both sides and no referral or franchise fees, $13,500 total commission × 70% − $295 = $9,155 estimated agent earnings before personal taxes and additional business expenses.


Can I calculate seller proceeds after commission?
Yes. Choose Seller Proceeds and optionally enter other closing costs and a mortgage payoff. The estimate subtracts the full entered commission, closing costs as a percentage of the sale price, and payoff. It is not a complete settlement statement or taxable profit calculation.


What fees can reduce a real estate agent’s earnings?
Referral fees, franchise or royalty fees, brokerage splits and transaction fees can reduce earnings. Team splits, broker caps, personal taxes and other business expenses are not modeled. A transaction fee larger than the agent share can produce a negative result.


What does my role in the deal change?
Listing agent uses the listing-side allocation; Buyer agent uses the remaining buyer-side allocation. Both sides uses the full commission remainder after referral and franchise fees, so the listing share does not affect that result.


Are referral and franchise fees stacked on each other?
No. Each percentage is calculated independently on total commission. Both amounts are deducted before allocating the remainder between the listing and buyer sides. Your actual compensation agreement may use a different order.
