How the commission estimate works

Total commission = sale price × total rate. Referral and franchise fees come off that total first, each calculated on the total — not stacked on each other. What remains is split between the listing side and the buyer side by your listing share. Your side depends on your role: listing agent, buyer agent, or both sides. Your share = your side × your agent split; your net subtracts the transaction fee. The seller view subtracts the full total commission, other closing costs and any mortgage payoff from the sale price.

Who is this for?

Agents estimating income from a sale, team members considering commission splits, and brokers checking a simple example scenario.

Real estate commission calculator: quick answer

Quick answer: A real estate commission calculator estimates the total commission on a sale, how it splits between the listing and buyer sides, your agent share after referral and franchise fees, and what the seller nets. On this page’s defaults, a $450,000 sale at 3% gives $13,500 total commission and, after a 70% split and a $295 transaction fee, $9,155 net to the agent.

A sale price is not an agent’s earnings. The total commission is split, fees come off, and only then does an agent’s share emerge. This calculator makes each of those steps visible, so a change in price, rate or split can be traced to the final figure instead of guessed.

Worked example

With the values already filled in — a $450,000 sale price, a 3% total commission rate, a 50% listing share, a 70% agent split and a $295 transaction fee, with no referral, franchise, other closing costs or mortgage payoff — the total commission is $13,500.00, your share before fees is $9,450.00, your net is $9,155.00, and the estimated net to the seller is $436,500.00. Referral and franchise fees, when entered, come off the total first, each calculated on the total rather than stacked on each other, and the remainder is split by the listing share.

Key takeaways

  • Start from the sale price and rate to get total commission, then apply fees, then the split — in that order.
  • Referral and franchise fees reduce the amount that is split, so they affect every side of the deal, not just one agent.
  • Your net is your side multiplied by your agent split, minus the transaction fee you enter.
  • The seller’s net subtracts the full total commission, other closing costs and any mortgage payoff from the sale price.

Frequently asked questions

How is real estate commission calculated?

Total commission equals sale price multiplied by total rate. Referral and franchise fees come off that total first, each on the total. The remainder is split between the listing and buyer sides by the listing share, and your share is your side multiplied by your agent split, minus the transaction fee.

What does my role in the deal change?

It decides which side’s commission is yours: listing agent, buyer agent, or both sides. With both sides, your side is the full remainder after fees; with one side, it is that side’s share of the remainder.

Are referral and franchise fees stacked on each other?

No. Each is calculated on the total commission, not on what remains after the other fee. That distinction matters when both percentages are entered.

What is not included in the agent net?

Taxes, team splits, broker caps and other business costs beyond the transaction fee you enter. A fee larger than your share can produce a negative result.

How is the seller’s net calculated?

Sale price minus the full total commission, minus other closing costs as a percentage of price, minus any mortgage payoff. Add closing costs or a payoff in the seller view to see it change.

Is the commission rate a standard rate?

No. Rates and splits vary by agreement, and the example figures here are sample defaults, not a standard or prescribed arrangement.

Limitations and next steps

Rates and splits vary by agreement. This model does not account for taxes, team splits, broker caps or other business costs beyond the fee you enter. Estimates only, not financial advice.
