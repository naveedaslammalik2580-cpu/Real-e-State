How the comparison works

Comparing monthly rent to a mortgage payment alone is misleading, so this calculator compares full multi-year costs, year by year up to 30 years. The net cost of buying is your down payment and closing costs plus every ownership payment so far — mortgage, property tax, insurance, HOA, PMI and maintenance — minus what selling that year would pocket: the home's value minus the remaining loan balance minus selling costs. The net cost of renting is all rent paid so far minus the investment growth on the down payment and closing costs you never spent. The breakeven year is the first year buying's net cost drops to or below renting's. Tax, insurance and maintenance are charged on the original price, PMI on the original loan while the balance is above 80% of the original price, and rent grows once a year.

What moves the answer

Three assumptions you cannot know in advance can each flip the result on their own: home price growth, the investment return a renter earns, and the mortgage rate. That is why the verdict is a test, not a promise — run it with pessimistic, expected and optimistic numbers. If buying wins in all three, it is a strong case; if it only wins in the optimistic run, you are betting on the market rather than on housing.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not a pre-approval, a lender quote or financial advice. Renter's insurance, moving costs and tax deductions are not counted; verify terms with your lender before making any buying decision.

Rent vs buy calculator: quick answer

Quick answer: A rent vs buy calculator compares the full multi-year cost of renting with the full cost of buying, year by year, and finds the first year buying becomes cheaper. On this page’s defaults, renting wins for a 7-year stay, and buying first wins in year 9.

Comparing a monthly rent figure with a mortgage payment alone is misleading. Buying also involves a down payment, closing costs, tax, insurance, HOA, PMI and maintenance, and later a sale with its own costs. Renting avoids those, but the money not spent on a down payment could otherwise be invested. This calculator puts both sides on the same multi-year footing.

Worked example

With the values already filled in — $1,800 monthly rent, a $350,000 home, $70,000 down, 6.5% over 30 years, a 7-year stay, 3% annual rent growth and 3% home price growth — the first-year monthly cost of owning is $2,561.46 against $1,800 rent now. Over the 7-year stay, the net cost of buying is $144,199.38 against a net renting cost of $132,737.60, so renting wins for that stay. The breakeven year is year 9, when buying’s net cost first drops to or below renting’s.

Key takeaways

  • Compare full multi-year costs, not just rent against a mortgage payment.
  • Buying rarely wins in the first few years, because closing and selling costs take time to earn back.
  • Home price growth, the investment return a renter earns, and the mortgage rate can each flip the answer on their own.
  • Run pessimistic, expected and optimistic numbers. Buying that wins in all three is a stronger case than buying that only wins when everything goes well.

Frequently asked questions

How does the rent vs buy comparison work?

The calculator tracks, year by year up to 30 years, the net cost of buying — down payment and closing costs plus ownership payments, minus what a sale that year would pocket — against the net cost of renting, which is rent paid minus investment growth on the money not spent buying.

What is the breakeven year?

The first year in which buying’s net cost drops to or below renting’s net cost. Before that year, renting is cheaper on these inputs; after it, buying is cheaper, assuming the inputs hold.

Why does buying lose at first even if the monthly payment looks close?

Closing costs when you buy and selling costs when you sell are large, one-off costs. They are spread over only a few years in a short stay, so they weigh heavily at first and fade over a longer stay.

What assumptions matter most?

Home price growth, the investment return a renter earns, and the mortgage rate. Tax, insurance and maintenance are charged on the original price in this model, PMI on the original loan while the balance is above 80% of that price, and rent grows once a year.

Does this calculator include taxes or moving costs?

No. Renter’s insurance, moving costs and tax deductions are not counted. It is an informational estimate, not a pre-approval, a lender quote or financial advice.

How long should I plan to stay for buying to make sense?

There is no universal answer — it depends on your rent, price, rate and growth assumptions. Enter your own stay length and read the breakeven year, rather than relying on a rule of thumb.

Limitations and next steps

This is an estimate based on the assumptions you enter, not a promise about future prices, rents or returns. Verify terms with your lender before making a buying decision.
