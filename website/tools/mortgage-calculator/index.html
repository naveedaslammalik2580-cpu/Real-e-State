How the payment is calculated

Monthly principal and interest follow the standard amortizing formula: M = P × r(1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments. Property tax and insurance are spread evenly across 12 months; HOA dues are added monthly; PMI is charged only while the balance is above 80% of the price. Any extra monthly payment and one-time payment go straight to principal, which shortens the loan and reduces total interest.

Mortgage calculator: quick answer, worked example and FAQs

Quick Answer: A mortgage calculator estimates your monthly payment from the loan amount, interest rate and term, then adds property tax, insurance, HOA dues and PMI where they apply. For the calculator's default $400,000 price, $80,000 down and 6.5% over 30 years, that total is $2,572.62 a month.

That total is the number to budget against — not just the principal-and-interest figure quoted in most rate comparisons. The rest of this section explains each part, works through the calculator's own default example step by step, and names what the estimate leaves out.

What does a mortgage calculator include?

A full monthly mortgage payment is usually more than principal and interest. The U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) describes the total as principal and interest, plus mortgage insurance where it applies, plus escrow for homeowners insurance and tax. This calculator follows that shape, and adds HOA dues as a separate monthly item:

  • Principal: the part of each payment that reduces the loan balance.
  • Interest: the lender's charge for borrowing, calculated each month on the remaining balance.
  • Property tax: your annual input divided by 12.
  • Home insurance: your annual input divided by 12.
  • HOA dues: your monthly input, added in full.
  • PMI: charged only while the down payment is below 20% — see the PMI section below for how this calculator's simplified rule differs from real cancellation rules.

Taxes and insurance do not reduce the loan balance. They are ownership costs collected alongside the loan payment in this estimate; whether a real lender collects them through escrow depends on the loan and the lender.

Worked example: the calculator's default inputs

InputValue

Down payment$80,000 (20%)

Step 1 — monthly rate and payment count. Monthly rate r = 6.5% ÷ 12 = 0.0054167. Number of payments n = 30 × 12 = 360.

Step 2 — principal and interest. M = P × r(1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), with P = $320,000. That gives an unrounded $2,022.6177 per month, displayed as $2,022.62 per month.

Step 3 — add the monthly ownership costs. As displayed: $2,022.62 + $400.00 tax + $150.00 insurance + $0 HOA + $0 PMI = $2,572.62 total per month. The unrounded total, $2,022.6177 + $550.00 = $2,572.6177, also displays as $2,572.62, so the displayed addition happens to match here.

Step 4 — totals over the term. Totals are calculated from the unrounded payment, not from the displayed monthly payment. Total principal and interest = $2,022.6177 × 360 = $728,142.36 as displayed (unrounded $728,142.3631). Do not write $2,022.62 × 360, which gives $728,143.20 — $0.84 higher. Total interest alone = $728,142.36 − $320,000 = $408,142.36. The calculator's "total of monthly payments over term" adds tax and insurance over 30 years as entered ($4,800 × 30 + $1,800 × 30 = $198,000), giving $926,142.36 — before the down payment, which is not part of that total. That figure is likewise unrounded-based; $2,022.62 × 360 + $198,000 would give $926,143.20.

Rounding rule for this whole section: monthly payments, interest, principal, and balances are shown rounded to cents, but every total, difference, and schedule figure in this calculator is calculated from the unrounded values first and rounded only for display. A displayed monthly payment multiplied by a displayed number of months will therefore not always equal a displayed total.

Early vs late payments. In month 1, interest is $320,000 × approximately 0.0054167 = $1,733.33 as displayed (unrounded $1,733.3333), and principal is $289.28 as displayed (unrounded $289.2843, that is, the unrounded payment minus the unrounded interest). Do not subtract the displayed figures: $2,022.62 − $1,733.33 = $289.29, which is $0.01 higher than the displayed principal, because both displayed inputs were rounded up. The balance after month 1 is $319,710.72. In year 1 as a whole, $20,694.69 goes to interest and $3,576.72 to principal, a sum of $24,271.41 — the unrounded monthly payment × 12 — not $2,022.62 × 12 = $24,271.44. That interest-heavy start is normal for a fixed-rate amortizing loan; the split reverses as the balance falls. Use the page's amortization schedule to see any year or month.

The 6.5% rate here is the calculator's sample default, not a current market rate and not a quote. Change it to any rate a lender has actually offered you before relying on the result.

A shorter term costs more each month but far less interest overall: on these defaults, 15 years instead of 30 adds $764.92 a month to principal and interest (the difference of the two displayed monthly payments, $2,787.54 − $2,022.62; the unrounded monthly difference is $764.93) and saves $226,384.52 in interest (the difference of the two displayed totals, $408,142.36 − $181,757.84, both calculated from unrounded payments). That trade-off is the real decision — not which term looks cheaper in a single month. Do not expect displayed monthly × months to equal a displayed total: for 15 years it gives $501,757.20 against a displayed total of $501,757.84; for 20 years $572,599.20 against $572,600.17; for 30 years $728,143.20 against $728,142.36. Tax, insurance, HOA, and PMI, where they apply, sit on top of each of these principal-and-interest figures.

What extra payments do in this calculator

An extra monthly payment and a one-time payment both go straight to principal in this calculator — the one-time payment is applied in the first month only. Extra principal shrinks the balance that future interest is charged on, so the loan can finish early and total interest can fall.

On the default loan, adding $200 a month with no other change finishes the loan in 281 months instead of 360, saves $105,428.67 in interest, and finishes 6 years 7 months sooner (the calculator's own "interest saved" and "time saved" outputs, cross-checked). Before committing to extra payments, check with your servicer how extra payments are applied — some servicers need an explicit "apply to principal" instruction, and this calculator cannot change a servicer's policy.

PMI in this calculator — and how real cancellation differs

This calculator uses a simplified PMI rule: if the down payment is below 20%, it charges PMI at your entered annual rate on the loan amount, divided by 12, and stops charging it once the balance is no longer above 80% of the home price. It does not model credit score, loan type, servicer rules, or property-value changes.

Example: a $400,000 price with 10% down ($40,000) gives a $360,000 loan. At 6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest is $2,275.44; at a 0.5% PMI rate, PMI is $150.00 a month; with the default $400 tax and $150 insurance, the total is $2,975.44 a month ($2,275.44 + $150.00 + $400.00 + $150.00). In this calculator, PMI runs for about 95 months on those inputs — an estimate of the simplified rule, not a promise about a real loan.

For a real U.S. conventional loan with borrower-paid PMI, the CFPB explains a different set of rights: you may ask your servicer in writing to cancel PMI when the balance is scheduled to reach 80% of the home's original value (or reaches it early through extra payments), subject to being current, having a good payment history, certifying no junior liens, and evidencing that the value has not fallen below the original value. Separately, the servicer must generally terminate PMI automatically when the balance is scheduled to reach 78% of the original value, if you are current. FHA and VA loans and lender-paid mortgage insurance follow different rules — ask your servicer which rules govern your loan before assuming PMI will stop at 80%.

What this calculator does not include

  • Closing costs, points, lender fees, and prepaid items. None are inputs, and none appear in any total.
  • The down payment itself in the "total of monthly payments" figure. It is cash paid at purchase, not a monthly payment.
  • Changes over time. Tax, insurance, and HOA are held flat at your entered values for the whole term in the totals. In real life they change, so a fixed-rate loan's principal-and-interest part can stay level while the total payment moves.
  • Adjustable rates. This is a fixed-rate calculator only. It cannot model a rate that resets.
  • Rate, approval, or eligibility. It does not check credit, income, debts, or property type, and it is not a loan offer.
  • Terms outside the buttons. The visible term choices are 15, 20, and 30 years; the underlying validation allows 1–50 years, but the page offers no other term control.
  • Escrow timing and servicer policy. The calculator divides annual tax and insurance by 12. It does not model escrow balances, shortages, or how a servicer applies extra payments.

That list is why the page already says "Estimates only. Not a loan offer or financial advice." Treat the output as a way to compare scenarios on equal inputs, then verify the scenario you actually intend to take with a lender, using a written Loan Estimate.

Key Takeaways

  • Budget on the full monthly total — principal, interest, tax, insurance, HOA, and PMI where it applies — not on principal and interest alone. On the default example, the difference is $550.00 a month.
  • On the default $320,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest is $2,022.62 a month as displayed (unrounded $2,022.6177), the full total is $2,572.62, and total interest is $408,142.36, calculated from the unrounded payment — not from $2,022.62 × 360, which would give $728,143.20.
  • A shorter term raises the monthly payment and cuts total interest sharply: 15 years saves $226,384.52 in interest against 30 years on these defaults (difference of the displayed totals), for $764.92 more principal and interest a month (difference of the displayed monthly payments; the unrounded monthly difference is $764.93).
  • Extra principal is powerful because it compounds in reverse: $200 a month extra on the default loan saves $105,428.67 and finishes 6 years 7 months early in this calculator.
  • The calculator's PMI rule is simplified. Real U.S. conventional PMI cancellation turns on the servicer's rules and, under federal law, the 80% request and 78% automatic-termination points described by the CFPB — not on this calculator's 80%-of-price switch alone.
  • Nothing here is a rate, a quote, or an approval. Tax, insurance, and HOA are your inputs, held flat in the totals, and closing costs are not included at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Principal and interest use the standard fixed-rate formula: payment = loan × monthly rate × (1 + monthly rate)payments ÷ ((1 + monthly rate)payments − 1). The monthly rate is the annual rate ÷ 12, and payments are years × 12. Tax and insurance are divided by 12, HOA is added monthly, and PMI is added where it applies.

What is included in the total monthly payment, beyond principal and interest?

The total adds property tax and home insurance (each annual figure ÷ 12), HOA dues in full each month, and PMI if the down payment is below 20%. The CFPB describes the same shape: principal and interest, plus mortgage insurance where applicable, plus escrow for insurance and tax.

What happens if I put less than 20% down?

With less than 20% down, this calculator adds PMI at your entered rate until the balance is no longer above 80% of the price. On a $400,000 home with 10% down at 6.5% over 30 years, that is a $360,000 loan, $2,275.44 principal and interest, $150.00 PMI, and a $2,975.44 total with default tax and insurance.

Does a shorter mortgage term always save money?

A shorter term saves total interest but raises the monthly payment, so it helps only if you can sustain that payment. On the default loan, 15 years costs $2,787.54 principal and interest against $2,022.62 for 30 years, but total interest falls from $408,142.36 to $181,757.84. Totals use the unrounded payment, not displayed monthly × months.

Do extra payments really save interest?

Yes, because extra principal reduces the balance that future interest is charged on. Adding $200 a month to the default loan finishes it in 281 months instead of 360, saves $105,428.67 in interest, and finishes 6 years 7 months sooner. The saving is largest early, when the balance and monthly interest charge are highest.

When can PMI actually be removed from a real loan?

For many U.S. conventional loans with borrower-paid PMI, the CFPB states you may request cancellation in writing at a scheduled 80% of original value, if current, with a good payment history, no junior liens, and evidence the value has not fallen. If you do nothing, termination is generally automatic at a scheduled 78%, if current.

Why can my total payment change if I have a fixed-rate loan?

The principal-and-interest part generally stays level, but tax, insurance, HOA dues, and escrow adjustments do not. Rising property values often raise taxes, premiums change, and an escrow review can move the escrow part of the payment. This calculator holds your entered tax, insurance, and HOA flat, so a real payment that moves is not a calculator error.

Is this mortgage calculator a loan offer or an approval?

No. It is an estimate from the numbers you enter only: price, down payment, rate, term, tax, insurance, HOA, PMI rate, and extra payments. It does not check credit, income, debts, property type, closing costs, or lender fees, and it uses the sample 6.5% default unless you change it.
