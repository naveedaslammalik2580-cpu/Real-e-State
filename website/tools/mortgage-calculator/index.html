How the mortgage payment is calculated

Principal and interest use the standard fixed-rate formula: M = P × r(1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the number of monthly payments. At 0% interest, the payment is simply P ÷ n. Property tax and home insurance are each divided by 12, HOA dues are added monthly, and PMI is added only while it applies. Extra monthly and one-time payments go straight to principal, which shortens the loan and reduces total interest.

The trade-off is simple: a shorter term costs more each month but far less interest overall, while a longer term lowers the monthly payment and raises total interest. Choose the term you can sustain, not just the one that looks cheapest in a single month.

Extra payments

Extra principal reduces the balance that future interest is charged on, so the loan can finish early. On the default loan, adding $200 a month with no other change finishes the loan in 281 months instead of 360, saves $105,428.67 in interest, and finishes 6 years 7 months sooner. Check with your servicer how extra payments are applied — some need an explicit “apply to principal” instruction.

PMI

This calculator uses a simplified PMI rule: if the down payment is below 20%, it charges PMI at your entered annual rate on the loan amount, divided by 12, and stops once the balance is no longer above 80% of the home price. For example, a $400,000 home with 10% down gives a $360,000 loan, $2,275.44 principal and interest, $150.00 PMI a month, and a $2,975.44 total with the default tax and insurance; PMI runs for about 95 months on those inputs. That is an estimate of the simplified rule, not a promise about a real loan. For many U.S. conventional loans with borrower-paid PMI, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) describes a different set of rights: you may request cancellation in writing at a scheduled 80% of the home's original value, subject to being current and other conditions, and the servicer must generally terminate PMI automatically at a scheduled 78% if you are current. FHA and VA loans and lender-paid mortgage insurance follow different rules.

Key takeaways

  • Budget on the full monthly total, not principal and interest alone — on the default example, the difference is $550.00 a month.
  • A shorter term raises the monthly payment and cuts total interest sharply; a longer term does the opposite.
  • Extra principal is powerful because it compounds in reverse: $200 a month extra saves $105,428.67 on the default loan.
  • The calculator's PMI rule is simplified; real cancellation depends on your servicer and the 80% request and 78% automatic-termination points described by the CFPB.

Frequently asked questions

How is a monthly mortgage payment calculated?

Principal and interest use the fixed-rate formula in “How the mortgage payment is calculated” above. Tax and insurance are divided by 12, HOA is added monthly, and PMI is added where it applies.

What is included in the total monthly payment?

Principal and interest, plus property tax, home insurance, HOA dues, and PMI if the down payment is below 20%. The CFPB describes the same shape: principal and interest, plus mortgage insurance where applicable, plus escrow for insurance and tax.

What happens if I put less than 20% down?

This calculator adds PMI at your entered rate until the balance is no longer above 80% of the price. On a $400,000 home with 10% down at 6.5% over 30 years, that is a $360,000 loan, $2,275.44 principal and interest, $150.00 PMI, and a $2,975.44 total with default tax and insurance.

Does a shorter mortgage term always save money?

It saves total interest but raises the monthly payment, so it helps only if you can sustain that payment. Compare the term table above on the same price, down payment and rate before deciding.

Do extra payments really save interest?

Yes, because extra principal reduces the balance that future interest is charged on. Adding $200 a month to the default loan finishes it in 281 months instead of 360 and saves $105,428.67 in interest.

When can PMI actually be removed from a real loan?

For many U.S. conventional loans with borrower-paid PMI, the CFPB states you may request cancellation in writing at a scheduled 80% of original value, if current and meeting the other conditions. If you do nothing, termination is generally automatic at a scheduled 78%, if current.

Why can my total payment change if I have a fixed-rate loan?

The principal-and-interest part generally stays level, but tax, insurance, HOA dues and escrow adjustments do not. This calculator holds your entered tax, insurance and HOA flat, so a real payment that moves is not a calculator error.

Is this mortgage calculator a loan offer or an approval?

No. It is an estimate from the numbers you enter only. It does not check credit, income, debts, property type, closing costs or lender fees, and it uses the sample 6.5% default unless you change it.

Limitations, sources and related calculators

This is an informational estimate, not a lender quote or financial advice. It does not include closing costs, points, lender fees or prepaid items; it holds tax, insurance and HOA flat over the whole term; it models fixed rates only, not adjustable rates; and it does not check credit, income, debts or property type.
