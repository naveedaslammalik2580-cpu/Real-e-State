How the analysis works

Start with the rent, subtract a vacancy allowance and add other income to get effective income. Subtract every operating expense — tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, CapEx reserve and management — to get net operating income (NOI). The mortgage never appears in NOI; it is counted separately as debt service. Monthly cash flow is effective income minus operating expenses minus the mortgage payment. Cap rate is NOI divided by price, cash-on-cash is annual cash flow divided by all the cash you invested (down payment plus closing costs), and DSCR is NOI divided by annual debt service — most lenders look for 1.25 or better.

What moves the answer

Rent and price dominate, but the quiet assumptions decide whether a deal survives contact with reality: vacancy, maintenance and CapEx reserves, and the management fee. Leaving any of them at zero flatters every deal — that is why this calculator keeps them visible instead of burying them. Break-even rent runs the math backwards and tells you the rent the property needs just to cover its costs; if your expected rent sits only just above it, the deal has no buffer for a vacancy or a repair.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not investment advice. It ignores appreciation, tax, principal paydown and resale costs, and a single year of rent and expenses is not a guarantee of the next. Verify rents, occupancy and costs with local records before making any buying decision.
