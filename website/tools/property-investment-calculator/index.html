Understanding the numbers

DSCR

Debt service coverage ratio is net operating income divided by annual debt service. Many lenders look for 1.25 or better, so the income covers the loan payment with a buffer.

Net operating income (NOI)

Effective income minus operating expenses, before the mortgage. It is the starting point for both cap rate and DSCR.

Break-even rent

The rent the property needs to cover operating expenses and the mortgage with zero cash flow. Any rent above it is your buffer against a vacancy or a repair.

Gross yield

Annual rent divided by price, before expenses. It is a quick first screen, not a return, because it ignores vacancy, expenses and financing.

How the analysis works

  1. Effective income. Take the rent, subtract a vacancy allowance, and add any other income.
  2. Net operating income (NOI). Subtract every operating expense: tax, insurance, HOA, maintenance, CapEx reserve and management. The mortgage is not included.
  3. Monthly cash flow. Subtract the mortgage payment from effective income minus operating expenses.
  4. Returns. Cap rate is NOI divided by price. Cash-on-cash is annual cash flow divided by all the cash you invested. DSCR is NOI divided by annual debt service, and most lenders look for 1.25 or better.

What moves the answer

Rent and price matter most, but the quiet assumptions decide whether a deal survives real life: vacancy, maintenance, CapEx reserves and the management fee. Setting any of them to zero flatters every deal, so this calculator keeps them visible.

Break-even rent runs the math backwards. If your expected rent is only just above it, the deal has no buffer for a vacancy or a repair.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not investment advice. It ignores appreciation, tax, principal paydown and resale costs, and one year of figures does not predict the next. Verify rents, occupancy and costs with local records before you buy.

Property investment calculator: quick answer

Quick answer: A property investment calculator, also called a rental property calculator, tests whether a rental deal pencils out: monthly cash flow, cash-on-cash return, cap rate, DSCR and break-even rent. On this page’s defaults, a $250,000 property with $2,400 rent gives $177.00 monthly cash flow and a 3.69% cash-on-cash return.

A deal can look good on rent alone and fail once vacancy, maintenance, reserves, management and the mortgage are counted. This calculator keeps those assumptions visible, so a deal is tested on its full costs, not just its headline rent.

Key takeaways

  • Cash flow is effective income minus operating expenses minus the mortgage payment. The mortgage never appears in NOI.
  • Cash-on-cash return uses all the cash invested: down payment plus closing costs.
  • DSCR is NOI divided by annual debt service. Most lenders want 1.25 or better.
  • Break-even rent is your buffer. If expected rent is only just above it, one vacancy or repair erases the profit.

Frequently asked questions

What is a rental property calculator?

It is the same kind of tool as this property investment calculator: it combines price, financing, rent and operating costs to show cash flow, return on cash invested, cap rate, DSCR and break-even rent for a rental property.

How is monthly cash flow calculated?

Rent minus a vacancy allowance, plus other income, gives effective income. Subtract operating expenses to get net operating income, then subtract the mortgage payment. What remains is monthly cash flow.

What is cash-on-cash return?

Annual cash flow divided by all the cash you invested, here down payment plus closing costs. It answers how hard your cash is working in the first year, not how the property performs over a whole hold.

What is DSCR and why does it matter?

Debt service coverage ratio is net operating income divided by annual debt service. Lenders use it to judge whether the property’s income comfortably covers the loan payment.

What is break-even rent?

The rent the property needs just to cover operating expenses and the mortgage payment, with zero cash flow. It is a useful stress test: the gap between expected rent and break-even rent is your buffer.

Does this calculator include appreciation or tax?

No. It ignores appreciation, tax, principal paydown and resale costs, and a single year of rent and expenses is not a guarantee of the next year. It is an informational estimate, not investment advice.

Limitations and next steps

Verify rents, occupancy and costs with local records before making any buying decision. A single year of figures does not predict the next year.
