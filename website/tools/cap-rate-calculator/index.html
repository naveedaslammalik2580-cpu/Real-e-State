How cap rate works

Cap rate = net operating income ÷ property price × 100. It measures the property as if you bought it in cash: start with the annual rent, subtract a vacancy allowance to get effective gross income, add any other income, then subtract every operating expense — property tax, insurance, maintenance, management and HOA. What is left is the net operating income (NOI). Because financing is deliberately excluded, cap rate lets you compare two properties on the asset alone, no matter how each deal is funded.

What moves the answer

Three inputs do most of the work: the rent you can actually collect, the vacancy you assume, and the price you pay — which is why the panel shows the cap rate at 10% below and above the asking price. A lower price lifts the cap rate without the property changing at all, so a high cap rate can mean a bargain or a property with problems; always check why the rent or the price looks the way it does. The implied value runs the formula backwards: NOI ÷ your target cap rate tells you the most the property is worth to you at that return.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not investment advice. Cap rate says nothing about your mortgage, taxes, appreciation or resale — and a single year of rent and expenses is not a guarantee of the next. Verify rents, occupancy and costs with local records before making any buying decision.
