How affordability is calculated

You state the monthly housing payment you can afford. The maximum price is solved directly from that payment: principal and interest on the loan, plus property tax, insurance, HOA and PMI, must add up to your budget. Because property tax is a percentage of the price and PMI is a percentage of the loan while the down payment is below 20%, the price is solved from the equation — not guessed. The loan is the price minus your down payment.

Estimating your budget from income (optional)

If you don't know your monthly budget, the guideline helper estimates one from your income and debts: the housing guideline limits housing costs to a share of gross monthly income, and the total-debt guideline limits housing plus other debts to a share of income. The lower of the two is the suggested budget. The familiar US defaults are 28% and 36%; other countries use different guidelines (for example, Canada commonly uses 32% and 44%), so enter your own if yours differ. The guideline only suggests a number — your monthly budget input always rules.

Understanding the estimate

Your actual qualification can differ due to credit score, employment history, loan program, local taxes and insurance, and lender rules. Just because a higher price fits a guideline does not mean it fits your budget; leave room for maintenance, savings and lifestyle costs.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not a pre-approval, a lender quote or financial advice. Verify terms with your lender before making any buying decision.
