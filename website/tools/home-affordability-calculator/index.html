How affordability is calculated

You state the monthly housing payment you can afford. The maximum price is solved directly from that payment: principal and interest on the loan, plus property tax, insurance, HOA and PMI, must add up to your budget. Because property tax is a percentage of the price and PMI is a percentage of the loan while the down payment is below 20%, the price is solved from the equation — not guessed. The loan is the price minus your down payment.

Estimating your budget from income (optional)

If you don't know your monthly budget, the guideline helper estimates one from your income and debts: the housing guideline limits housing costs to a share of gross monthly income, and the total-debt guideline limits housing plus other debts to a share of income. The lower of the two is the suggested budget. The familiar US defaults are 28% and 36%; other countries use different guidelines (for example, Canada commonly uses 32% and 44%), so enter your own if yours differ. The guideline only suggests a number — your monthly budget input always rules.

Understanding the estimate

Your actual qualification can differ due to credit score, employment history, loan program, local taxes and insurance, and lender rules. Just because a higher price fits a guideline does not mean it fits your budget; leave room for maintenance, savings and lifestyle costs.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not a pre-approval, a lender quote or financial advice. Verify terms with your lender before making any buying decision.

Home affordability calculator: quick answer

Quick answer: A home affordability calculator starts from the monthly housing payment you can afford and works backwards to the maximum home price, loan and payment breakdown, with property tax, insurance, HOA and PMI included. On this page’s defaults, a $2,300 monthly budget with a $60,000 down payment supports a maximum price of $330,118.43.

Most buyers start with a price and hope the payment fits. This tool reverses the question: set the payment you can actually sustain, then see what price that payment supports. That keeps taxes, insurance, HOA and PMI in the answer, instead of discovering them after you have already fallen for a house.

Worked example

With the values already filled in when the page loads — a $2,300 monthly budget, $60,000 down, 6.5% over 30 years, a 1.2% property tax rate, $1,800 annual insurance, no HOA and a 0.5% PMI rate — the calculator gives a maximum price of $330,118.43, a maximum loan of $270,118.43 and principal and interest of $1,707.33 a month. The price is solved directly from the payment equation, not guessed, because tax scales with price and PMI scales with the loan while the down payment is below 20%.

The optional income guideline is separate from that budget. With $100,000 gross annual income, $500 of other monthly debts and the familiar 28% housing and 36% total-debt guidelines, it suggests $2,333.33 a month — the lower of the two guideline limits. Your monthly budget input always rules over the guideline.

Key takeaways

  • Start from a payment you can sustain, not from the highest price a lender mentions.
  • Tax, insurance, HOA and PMI are part of the payment here, so the maximum price already reflects them.
  • A higher budget, a lower rate or a larger down payment raises the maximum price; a higher tax rate or PMI rate lowers it.
  • A guideline price is not a pre-approval. Credit, employment, loan program and lender rules can change the real answer.

Frequently asked questions

How does a home affordability calculator work?

You enter the monthly housing payment you can afford, plus down payment, rate, term and ownership costs. The calculator solves for the price at which principal and interest, tax, insurance, HOA and PMI add up to that budget, then shows the loan and the payment breakdown.

What is included in the monthly payment?

Principal and interest, property tax, home insurance, HOA dues and PMI where it applies. Closing costs are shown separately as estimated cash to close, not as part of the monthly payment.

Why is the maximum price lower than price divided by something simple?

Because the payment must cover more than the loan. Tax is a percentage of price and PMI is a percentage of the loan, so both grow with the price and must be solved for together.

Should I use the income guideline budget?

Use it as a starting point if you do not know your budget. It applies the housing and total-debt percentages you enter to your income and debts, and takes the lower result. Adjust it to your real spending, savings and lifestyle costs.

Does this calculator pre-approve me for a loan?

No. It is an informational estimate, not a pre-approval, a lender quote or financial advice. Verify terms with your lender before making a buying decision.

What if I can afford more than the guideline?

Enter your own monthly budget directly. The guideline only suggests a number; the budget input is what drives the maximum price.

Limitations and next steps

This estimate does not check credit score, employment history, loan program, local tax and insurance changes, or lender rules, and it holds your entered tax, insurance and HOA figures as given. Just because a price fits a guideline does not mean it fits your budget — leave room for maintenance, savings and lifestyle costs.
