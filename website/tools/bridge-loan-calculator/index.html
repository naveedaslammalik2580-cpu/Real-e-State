How a bridge loan works

A bridge loan lets you buy the new home before the old one sells, using the equity in your current home as security. The maximum you can borrow is your current home's value times the lender's loan limit, minus your existing mortgage balance. Payments are interest-only — you pay just the monthly interest while the loan runs, and the full principal comes due when your current home sells or the term ends, whichever comes first. Rates run higher than a conventional mortgage because the loan is short and the lender carries two properties' worth of risk at once.

What moves the answer

Two inputs decide almost everything: the loan limit and your existing mortgage. If your mortgage already sits near the limit, little or no bridge loan is available at all — the panel says so plainly instead of showing a negative loan. After that, time is the cost: every extra month until the sale adds one more interest-only payment, so a 12-month sale costs roughly twice the interest of a 6-month one while the fees stay fixed. That is why fees weigh heaviest on small, short bridge loans.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not a loan offer or financial advice. Lender limits, rates and fees vary, and if your current home does not sell within the term you may need an extension or a refinance — both usually cost more. Confirm terms with your lender before relying on a bridge loan to close.

Bridge loan calculator: quick answer

Quick answer: A bridge loan calculator estimates how much you can borrow against your current home’s equity to buy before you sell, what it costs, and whether it covers your new down payment. On this page’s defaults, a $450,000 home with a $200,000 mortgage supports a maximum bridge loan of $160,000.

Buying before selling solves a timing problem, but it creates a short, usually interest-only loan that must be repaid when the old home sells or the term ends. The key question is not just how much you can borrow, but how much it costs for every month the sale takes.

Worked example

With the values already filled in — a $450,000 current value, a $200,000 mortgage, a $600,000 new price, a 20% down payment, an 80% bridge limit, a 9% rate, 6 months until sale, a 1% origination fee, $3,000 other closing costs and 6% selling costs — equity is $250,000, the maximum bridge loan is $160,000, the interest-only payment is $1,200 a month, and total bridge cost is $11,800, made up of $7,200 interest, $1,600 origination and $3,000 other closing costs. The down payment needed is $120,000, fully covered by the bridge loan, and cash left after the current home sells is $63,000.

Key takeaways

  • Maximum bridge loan equals current value multiplied by the lender limit, minus your existing mortgage.
  • Payments are interest-only, so the principal is still due in full when the old home sells or the term ends.
  • Time is the main cost: a 12-month sale costs roughly twice the interest of a 6-month sale, while fees stay fixed.
  • If your mortgage already sits near the limit, little or no bridge loan may be available — the calculator says so plainly instead of showing a negative loan.

Frequently asked questions

How does a bridge loan work?

You borrow against the equity in your current home to buy the new home before the old one sells. Payments are usually interest-only while the loan runs, and the full principal comes due when the current home sells or the term ends, whichever comes first.

How is the maximum bridge loan calculated?

Current home value multiplied by the bridge loan limit, minus the existing mortgage balance. In the worked example, $450,000 at 80% is $360,000, minus a $200,000 mortgage, giving $160,000.

What does a bridge loan cost?

Monthly interest on the loan, plus origination and other closing costs. Rates run higher than a conventional mortgage because the loan is short and the lender carries two properties’ worth of risk at once.

Will the bridge loan cover my new down payment?

That depends on the loan amount against the down payment needed. Here a $120,000 down payment is fully covered by a $160,000 bridge loan; in other cases cash may still be needed, and the calculator shows that amount.

What happens if my current home does not sell in time?

You may need an extension or a refinance, both usually costing more. Confirm terms with your lender before relying on a bridge loan to close.

Is this a loan offer?

No. This is an informational estimate, not a loan offer or financial advice. Lender limits, rates and fees vary.

Limitations and next steps

This estimate does not model credit, loan type, servicer rules or property-value changes, and it assumes the sale price equals the current value entered. Confirm terms with your lender before relying on a bridge loan.
