How a bridge loan works

A bridge loan lets you buy the new home before the old one sells, using the equity in your current home as security. The maximum you can borrow is your current home's value times the lender's loan limit, minus your existing mortgage balance. Payments are interest-only — you pay just the monthly interest while the loan runs, and the full principal comes due when your current home sells or the term ends, whichever comes first. Rates run higher than a conventional mortgage because the loan is short and the lender carries two properties' worth of risk at once.

What moves the answer

Two inputs decide almost everything: the loan limit and your existing mortgage. If your mortgage already sits near the limit, little or no bridge loan is available at all — the panel says so plainly instead of showing a negative loan. After that, time is the cost: every extra month until the sale adds one more interest-only payment, so a 12-month sale costs roughly twice the interest of a 6-month one while the fees stay fixed. That is why fees weigh heaviest on small, short bridge loans.

Important note

This is an informational estimate, not a loan offer or financial advice. Lender limits, rates and fees vary, and if your current home does not sell within the term you may need an extension or a refinance — both usually cost more. Confirm terms with your lender before relying on a bridge loan to close.
