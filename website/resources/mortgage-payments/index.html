Understanding Mortgage Payments

The Mortgage Calculator estimates the monthly cost of a fixed-rate, amortizing loan. Its headline figure is a total monthly payment — principal and interest, plus property tax, homeowner insurance, HOA dues and PMI whenever you enter them. This guide explains each part, starting with the principal-and-interest calculation at its core.

The terms behind the estimate

Principal
The amount borrowed: home price minus down payment.
Down payment
The portion of the home price paid upfront in this model.
Interest rate
The entered annual percentage rate of interest, divided by 12 for the monthly calculation. Other loan costs are not modeled.
Loan term
The number of years over which the model repays the loan; years × 12 gives the monthly payment count.
Property tax
An annual amount you can enter. The calculator divides it by 12 and adds it to the monthly total.
Homeowner insurance
An annual amount you can enter, also divided by 12 and added to the monthly total.
HOA dues
A monthly amount you can enter, added to the monthly total as entered.
Private mortgage insurance (PMI)
An estimated monthly charge while the down payment is under 20% of the price: principal × the PMI rate you enter ÷ 12. In this model it stops once the loan balance reaches 80% of the home price.

Worked example

Worked example

From home price to monthly payment

Inputs

Use a $400,000 home price, an $80,000 down payment, 6.5% annual interest and a 30-year term.

Calculation
  1. Principal: $400,000 − $80,000$320,000.00
  2. Monthly rate: 6.5 ÷ 1,2000.00541667 (rounded)
  3. Payments: 30 × 12360 months
  4. ResultEstimated monthly principal + interest$2,022.62
Explanation

The calculation uses the unrounded monthly rate; displayed currency rounds to cents.

See the payment formula

M = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1).

P is principal, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of payments. At 0% interest, the calculator uses M = P ÷ n. Total paid is M × n; total interest is that amount minus principal.

Reading the result: this example enters no property tax, insurance, HOA dues or PMI, so the calculator's total monthly payment equals the principal-and-interest figure above. Enter those costs and the total rises by their monthly amounts — a principal-and-interest-only figure is not the full monthly cost of owning the home.

What changes the result?

With other inputs unchanged, a larger down payment reduces principal and a higher interest rate increases the payment. At a positive fixed rate, a longer term lowers the monthly payment but increases total interest in this model.

The optional costs are added the same way every time: property tax and homeowner insurance as one-twelfth of the annual amounts you enter, HOA dues as the monthly amount you enter, and PMI while the down payment is under 20% of the price. Optional extra monthly payments and a one-time lump sum are also modeled — they shorten the payoff schedule and reduce total interest.

What is not included?

The estimate only covers the amounts you enter. It does not include closing costs or lender fees such as origination charges, and it does not model rate changes — the entered rate stays fixed for the whole term. It is an educational estimate, not a lender quote.
